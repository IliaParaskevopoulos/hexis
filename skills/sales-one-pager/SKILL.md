# Sales one-pager

Goal: one page a seller leaves behind or sends after a meeting, that a buyer understands in 30 seconds and can forward to colleagues who weren't there. One page per product, or one per buyer role for the same product. It must work printed (A4 and US Letter) and as a PDF attachment.

What makes a one-pager work:
- **It sells one thing to one reader.** One product, one buyer role, one main message. Everything else goes.
- **It's built for forwarding.** The champion passes it to people who never met the seller, so it must explain itself without a pitch.
- **Scannable in 30 seconds.** Headline, three benefits, proof, next step. Long paragraphs don't get read.
- **Proof beats claims.** A number, a named customer, a certification or a study does more than any adjective.
- **Same story everywhere.** Words come from the messaging house, so the page matches the website, deck and what sellers say.

## 1. Brief (ask only what's missing, max 4 questions, in one message)
Inputs:
- **Product or offer** (website, deck, spec sheet)
- **Messaging house** if it exists (use it as the source of truth; if not, build a short one first with the messaging-house skill, or derive roof, 3 pillars and proof from the website and say so)
- **Reader**: which buyer role(s) the page is for (e.g., Head of R&D, CFO, procurement, end user). One page per role if several. Use the ICP and buyer personas board if it exists.
- **Industry** (optional). If named, apply the industry lens in step 3; if not, stay industry-neutral and say so.
- **Use**: left behind after a meeting, sent as a PDF, handed out at a trade show, or included in a proposal
- **Brand**: logo, colours, fonts, contact details, and the call to action (book a call, request samples, start a trial)

If nobody is there to answer, make reasonable assumptions, list them at the top, and continue.

## 2. Pick the message for this reader
From the messaging house and persona:
- **Headline**: the main benefit for this reader, in their words, 10 words or fewer. Not the product name, not a slogan.
- **Subhead**: one sentence on what the product is and who it's for.
- **The problem**: 1–2 sentences the reader recognises from their own day.
- **Three benefits**: the three pillars, re-ordered and reworded for this reader. Each a short bold line plus one sentence.
- **Proof**: one proof point per benefit, plus one strong proof item for the page (customer quote with name and role, result number, certification, study, award).
- **Why us vs. the alternative**: 2–3 short contrasts against the buyer's real alternative (a competitor type, doing it in-house, or doing nothing), fair and true.
- **Next step**: one clear call to action, with a contact name, email, phone and a QR code or short link.

Persona variants change the headline, benefit order, proof and call to action, never the facts.

## 3. Industry lens (only when an industry is named)
- **Regulated (food supplements, pharma, medical devices, finance)**: only approved or substantiated claims; for health products, no disease claims; add required statements and references in small print; mark the page for the company's own review before use.
- **Ingredients, chemicals, manufacturing**: add a short specs box (dose, form, certifications, origin, shelf life, MOQ, lead time) and the document pack available on request.
- **Software and SaaS**: integrations, security and compliance badges, time to value, pricing model.
- **Professional services**: method in 3 steps, team credentials, typical engagement and results.
- **Public sector**: framework agreements, references in the public sector, compliance.

State the lens in the brief: "Industry lens: [industry]" or "Industry-neutral".

## 4. Layout (one page, one column or a simple two-column grid)
Top to bottom:
1. **Header band**: logo, product name, the reader it's for (small: "For R&D teams").
2. **Headline and subhead.**
3. **The problem** (short).
4. **Three benefits**: three blocks side by side or stacked, each with a simple line icon, bold benefit, one sentence, one proof line.
5. **Proof strip**: one customer quote or key number, large; plus logos, certifications or study references if available.
6. **Why us**: 2–3 contrasts, or a small comparison against the alternative (avoid big feature tables).
7. **Specs or key facts** box when the industry lens calls for it.
8. **Call to action and contact**: one action, a named contact, email, phone, QR code to the right page.
9. **Footer**: small print, references, date or version, legal lines.

Design rules:
- One idea per block, lots of white space, body text at least 9 pt printed (12 px on screen), headline 24–32 pt.
- Brand colours of the company if known; otherwise Byzantine purple (#702963), near-black (#1F1A1E), light tint (#F6F1F5) and Montserrat.
- Real proof only; no stock photos of handshakes, no generic icons that say nothing, no emoji.
- Fits exactly one A4 page and one US Letter page with safe margins (at least 12 mm); nothing cut off when printed.
- Readable in black and white; contrast at least 4.5:1.
- QR code must be real and point to the stated link; test it.

## 5. Write it well
- Plain words the reader uses. Short sentences. Benefits before features.
- Specific beats general: "cuts approval time from 6 weeks to 2" beats "faster approvals".
- No buzzwords ("cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "leverage", "seamless", "solutions").
- Never invent numbers, customers, quotes, logos or certifications. Missing proof becomes a visible placeholder like [Customer result needed] so it can't be printed by mistake.

## 6. Build and deliver
- Use a Design artifact type if listed (one artboard per page, sized for A4 and Letter, print-ready); otherwise one HTML artifact with print CSS (`@page` size A4/Letter, no browser margins).
- One page per product or persona, shown side by side so variants can be compared.
- Offer a PDF of each page (A4 and Letter) and an editable version.
- Name files clearly: `[company]-[product]-[persona]-one-pager-[YYYY-MM].pdf`.

## 7. Check before showing
- [ ] One reader, one main message; headline is a benefit in the reader's words
- [ ] Three benefits match the messaging house pillars, each with proof
- [ ] At least one strong proof item (quote, number, certification, study); nothing invented
- [ ] One call to action with a named contact and a working QR code or link
- [ ] Fits one printed page in A4 and Letter; nothing cut off; readable in black and white
- [ ] Industry lens applied if named; claims allowed for that industry
- [ ] Persona variants differ in emphasis, not in facts
- [ ] 30-second test: someone new to the product can say what it is, for whom, and why it's better

## 8. Handoff
In the reply: which pages were made (product × persona), the headline of each, the proof still missing, and where each page is used (leave-behind, email follow-up, trade show, proposal). Offer one next step: a follow-up email that carries the one-pager, a matching trade-show version, or a two-page version with a case study on the back.
