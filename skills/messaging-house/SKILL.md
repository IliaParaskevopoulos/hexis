# Messaging house

Goal: one page that tells everyone (sales, marketing, the website, the CEO) what to say about a company or product, in what order, with proof. It turns a positioning into words people can reuse. The output is a messaging house visual plus copy-ready text.

## 1. Start from the positioning
- If a positioning already exists (from the positioning-map skill or the client), use it. Take the target buyer, the positioning phrase and the proven differences.
- If not, fetch the company's website, deck and case studies and ask, in one message, max 4 questions: who the buyer is, the main alternative they compare against, the top 3 reasons customers choose them, and the proof they can share (numbers, customers, certifications, awards).
- If nobody is there to answer, make reasonable assumptions, list them at the top, and continue.

## 2. Build the house
**Roof: the core message**
- One sentence (max 20 words) that says what the company does for whom and why it's better. It follows from the positioning phrase.
- Optional: 2 tagline options, max 6 words each.

**Pillars: 3 value pillars (4 at most)**
Each pillar is one reason to choose the company, tied to a differentiation factor. For each pillar:
- **Headline**: 3–7 words, a benefit for the buyer, not a feature.
- **Message**: 1–2 sentences explaining it in plain words.
- **Proof points**: 2–4 facts that make it believable: numbers, named customers, certifications, patents, test results, quotes. Each with its source. Missing proof becomes a visible placeholder like [Customer result needed].
- **Features behind it**: the product features or capabilities that deliver this benefit.

Pillars must not overlap. If two pillars can be explained with the same proof, merge them.

**Foundation**
- Who they are in one line (founded, where, size, credentials)
- Brand personality: 3 words, each with a "this, not that" (e.g. "Precise, not cold")
- Tone of voice: 3 short do/don't rules

## 3. Make it usable
- **Persona variants**: for 2–4 buyer roles (e.g. CEO, R&D lead, procurement), say which pillar leads, what they care about, the one line to open with and the likely objection with a reply.
- **Elevator pitches**: 10 seconds (one sentence), 30 seconds (3–4 sentences), 2 minutes (a short paragraph structure: problem, what we do, why us, proof, ask).
- **Boilerplate**: a 50-word and a 100-word "About [company]" for press releases and profiles.
- **Words to use / words to avoid**: 5 each, from the company's own world.

## 4. Write it well
- Plain, human language a buyer would say. Short sentences.
- No buzzwords: "unlock", "leverage", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "solutions", "empower", "in today's fast-paced world".
- Benefits before features. Specific beats general: "cuts capsule size variation to under 3%" beats "unmatched precision".
- Never invent claims, numbers, customers or quotes. Unproven claims are marked as hypotheses.

## 5. Visual
Build one HTML artifact:
- A house drawn in simple shapes: the roof (core message) on top, the 3–4 pillars side by side (headline, message, proof points), the foundation as a band at the bottom. Brand colours of the client if known, otherwise Byzantine purple (#702963) and Montserrat. Flat, minimal, lots of white space; no icons, no gradients, no stock imagery.
- Below the house: persona variants, pitches, boilerplate and word lists as clean text sections.
- A "Copy" button on each block so text can be pasted into decks, websites and emails.
- If a Docs artifact type is listed and the person wants an editable document too, also offer the text version there.

## 6. Check before showing
- [ ] The roof follows from the positioning and names the buyer and the difference
- [ ] 3–4 pillars, no overlap, each tied to a differentiation factor
- [ ] Every pillar has at least 2 proof points with sources, or visible placeholders
- [ ] Competitor swap test: could a competitor put its name on the roof or a pillar? If yes, sharpen it
- [ ] Read-aloud test: every line sounds natural when said to a buyer
- [ ] No buzzwords from the list above
- [ ] Pitches and boilerplate say the same thing as the house, in the same order

## 7. Handoff
In the reply: the core message, the 3 pillar headlines, and the proof that is still missing. Offer one next step: a sales battlecard, website copy, or a one-page sales sheet built from this house.
