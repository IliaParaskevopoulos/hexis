# Messaging architecture

Goal: one system that tells everyone (sales, marketing, the website, the CEO, agencies) what to say, to whom, at which stage, in which length, and with what proof. It connects the company promise to every product and feature, so the same story holds from a tagline to a proposal. The messaging house sits at its centre.

The output is one artifact with seven sections:
1. Message map (brand promise → portfolio pillars → products → features)
2. Messaging house for the focus product (roof, pillars with proof, foundation)
3. Persona layer
4. Journey layer
5. Proof library
6. Channel versions, pitches and boilerplate
7. Words, tone and governance

## 1. Brief (ask only what's missing, max 4 questions, in one message)
Start from what exists: positioning (from the positioning or positioning-map skill), ICP and buyer personas, value proposition, website, deck, case studies. Fetch the website and read it first. Then ask only for what you can't find:
- **Scope**: the whole company, one portfolio area, or one product (the **focus product** gets the full messaging house)
- **Buyers**: the target buyer and the roles in the buying committee
- **Main alternative** buyers compare against (a competitor type, doing it in-house, or doing nothing)
- **Proof** they can share: numbers, named customers, certifications, studies, awards
- **Industry** (optional). If named, apply the industry lens in step 2. If not, stay industry-neutral and say so.

If nobody is there to answer, make reasonable assumptions, list them at the top, and continue.

## 2. Industry lens (only when an industry is named)
- **Regulated (food supplements, pharma, medical devices, finance)**: only approved or substantiated claims; no disease claims for health products; on-pack, B2B and science messages kept apart; every claim needs regulatory sign-off.
- **Ingredients, chemicals, manufacturing**: technical buyers first; specs, dose, origin, certifications, supply security and documentation carry the proof.
- **Software and SaaS**: product-led vs sales-led messages; integrations, security, time to value; feature names consistent with the product UI.
- **Professional services**: method, people and outcomes instead of features.
- **Consumer brands selling through retail**: separate trade messages (margin, sell-through) from consumer messages.

## 3. Message map (the hierarchy)
Build four levels. Each level must support the one above it; nothing at a lower level may contradict a higher one.
- **Brand promise**: one sentence on what the company stands for and for whom, plus one line explaining it. True for every product.
- **Portfolio pillars**: 2–4 areas (by need, segment or product line), each with a one-line message.
- **Product messages**: one line per product: what it is, for whom, the main difference.
- **Features → what they mean for the buyer**: for the focus product, 4–6 features, each translated into a buyer benefit.

Mark gaps as visible placeholders ([Product message]). Fade branches that are out of scope in the visual.

## 4. Messaging house (focus product)
**Roof: the core message**
- One sentence (max 20 words): what it does, for whom, and why it's better. It follows from the positioning.
- 2 tagline options, max 6 words each.

**Pillars: 3 value pillars (4 at most)**
Each pillar is one reason to choose the product, tied to a differentiation factor:
- **Headline**: 3–7 words, a buyer benefit, not a feature
- **Message**: 1–2 sentences in plain words
- **Proof points**: 2–4 facts with sources (numbers, customers, certifications, patents, studies, quotes); missing proof becomes [Customer result needed]
- **Features behind it**: the capabilities that deliver this benefit (linked to the message map)

Pillars must not overlap. If two pillars rely on the same proof, merge them.

**Foundation**
- Who they are in one line (founded, where, size, credentials)
- Brand personality: 3 words, each with "this, not that" (e.g., "Precise, not cold")
- Tone of voice: 3 short do/don't rules

## 5. Persona layer
For each buying-committee role (usually 3–5):
- **Lead message**: which pillar leads for them, in their words
- **Supporting point**
- **Proof to show**
- **Likely objection and a one-line reply**
Same facts for everyone; only emphasis and order change.

## 6. Journey layer
For each stage (awareness, consideration, evaluation, decision, launch and growth):
- **Message** for that stage, in one line
- **Carried by**: the content and channels that carry it (posts, website, one-pager, demo, document pack, proposal, onboarding)
Highlight the stage where deals usually stall and make sure it has a message and content.

## 7. Proof library
A table of every claim used anywhere in the architecture:
- **Claim** | **Proof and source** | **Status** (Use / Confirm first / Don't use) | **Where it may appear**
Only "Use" claims go into customer materials. Unproven or legally risky claims are listed as "Don't use", with the reason, so nobody reuses them by accident.

## 8. Channel versions, pitches and boilerplate
For the focus product:
- **Tagline** (≤ 6 words), **25 words** (ads, email preview), **50 words** (website hero, social), **100 words** (one-pager, deck, proposal). Each longer version expands the shorter one; same order, same facts.
- **Elevator pitches**: 10 seconds (one sentence), 30 seconds (3–4 sentences), 2 minutes (problem, what we do, why us, proof, ask).
- **Boilerplate**: 50-word and 100-word "About [company]" for press releases and profiles.

## 9. Words, tone and governance
- **Words to use / avoid**: 5 each, from the company's own world, including naming rules (first mention vs short name, old names to retire).
- **Governance**: owner; who signs off new claims; review cadence (quarterly) and triggers for an immediate update (new product, new proof, a claim challenged, a competitor move); the checks every new asset must pass.

## 10. Write it well
- Plain, human language a buyer would say. Short sentences. Benefits before features.
- Specific beats general: "cuts capsule size variation to under 3%" beats "unmatched precision".
- No buzzwords: "unlock", "leverage", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "solutions", "empower", "game-changer".
- Never invent claims, numbers, customers or quotes. Unknowns become visible [placeholders].

## 11. Visual
One artifact (use a Docs or Design type if listed and it fits; otherwise one HTML artifact), with a section navigation on top and a distinct header per section. Brand colours of the client if known, otherwise Byzantine purple (#702963), near-black (#1F1A1E), light tint (#F6F1F5) and Montserrat. Flat, minimal, lots of white space; no stock images, gradients or emoji. Force light colours so dark-mode viewers don't turn text white.
- **Message map**: a top-down tree (brand promise → pillars → products → features), focus branch highlighted, others faded.
- **Messaging house**: drawn as a house: roof on top, pillars side by side, foundation band at the bottom.
- **Persona layer**: a table (role, lead message, supporting point, proof, objection).
- **Journey layer**: a row of stages with the stall stage highlighted.
- **Proof library**: a table with coloured status tags.
- **Channel versions**: tagline to 100 words, stacked, with word counts.
- A "Copy" button on every message block.

## 12. Check before showing
- [ ] Every level of the map supports the one above; no contradictions
- [ ] The roof follows from the positioning and names the buyer and the difference
- [ ] 3–4 pillars, no overlap, each with at least 2 sourced proof points or visible placeholders
- [ ] Every claim in every section appears in the proof library with a status
- [ ] Persona and journey messages use the same facts as the house
- [ ] Channel versions, pitches and boilerplate say the same thing in the same order
- [ ] Competitor swap test: a rival couldn't put its name on the roof, a pillar or the tagline
- [ ] Read-aloud test passes; no buzzwords
- [ ] Industry lens applied if named; otherwise marked industry-neutral

## 13. Handoff
In the reply: the brand promise, the focus product's core message and pillar headlines, the claims marked "Confirm first" or "Don't use", and the proof still missing. Offer one next step: website copy, a sales one-pager, battlecards, or a first-meeting sales deck built from this architecture.
