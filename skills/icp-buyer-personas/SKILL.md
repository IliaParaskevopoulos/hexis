# ICP and buyer personas

Goal: one visual board that tells sales and marketing exactly which companies to target, who inside them decides, how they buy, and how to reach them. The input is a company's website or deck. The output is an ideal customer profile (ICP), a buying committee map, a buying journey, and one persona card per key role.

## 1. Brief (ask only what's missing, max 3 questions, in one message)
Fetch the website or read the deck first. Pull out: what they sell, price level, current customers and logos, case studies, industries named, regions, team size. Then ask only for what you can't find:
- The product or offer to profile (if there are several)
- Their best 3–5 current customers, and why they are the best (fast close, big deal, low churn)
- Any segment they want to enter that they don't serve yet

If nobody is there to answer, make reasonable assumptions, list them at the top, and continue.

## 2. Research
- Read the company's case studies, customer logos, testimonials, job posts and pricing page. Customers they already win are the strongest evidence for the ICP.
- Look at who competitors target (their case studies, industries pages, ads).
- Find what buyers in this category say: reviews (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Google), forums, LinkedIn posts, trade press, analyst reports.
- Check job postings at target companies: the roles they hire and the tools they list show who owns the problem.
- Keep a source next to every fact. Anything not backed by evidence is marked as a hypothesis.

## 3. Ideal customer profile (the company)
Write firmographics as a short scorecard:
- **Industry and sub-segment** (specific: "mid-size contract manufacturers of food supplements", not "healthcare")
- **Company size**: employees and revenue band
- **Region and language**
- **Business model and maturity** (e.g. B2B, growing, post-Series A, family-owned)
- **Technographics**: tools or systems they already use that make them a fit
- **Situation signals**: what is true about a company that needs this now (new regulation, expansion, a new site, hiring for X)
- **Disqualifiers**: who looks like a fit but isn't (too small, wrong model, in-house team already)

Add a fit score: 3–5 criteria, each weighted, so sales can score a lead in one minute.

## 4. Buying committee
Map 4–7 roles that take part in the decision. For each role, state:
- **Role in the deal**: Decision maker, Champion, User, Technical evaluator, Financial approver (procurement/finance), Influencer (internal or external), Blocker
- **Power** (high/medium/low) and **interest** (high/medium/low)
- What they need to say yes

Include external influencers: consultants, advisors, industry associations, peers, analysts, regulators, review sites. Influencers often decide who gets shortlisted.

## 5. Buying journey
Lay out 5–6 stages, in the buyer's words:
1. **Trigger**: something happens that makes the problem urgent
2. **Problem framing**: they name the problem and its cost
3. **Search**: they look for options
4. **Evaluation and shortlist**: demos, samples, references, RFPs
5. **Decision and approval**: business case, procurement, legal
6. **Onboarding and expansion**: first result, then more

For each stage: which roles are active, what they do, the questions they ask, the content or proof that helps, where they look, and the typical length of the stage. Mark the stage where deals usually stall.

## 6. Persona cards (one per key role, usually 3–4)
Each card has:
- **Name the role, not a fake person** ("Head of R&D, mid-size supplement brand"). No invented names, stock photos or ages.
- **Goals**: what success looks like for them, in their words
- **Pains**: 3–5 specific frustrations, each with the evidence behind it
- **Triggers**: events that make them act now
- **Objections**: 3 likely objections, each with a short reply
- **Decision criteria**: what they compare on
- **Where to find them**: specific channels, events, trade shows, publications, LinkedIn groups, communities, podcasts, associations, search terms. Name them; "social media" is not an answer.
- **Opening line**: one sentence a salesperson can open with

## 7. Write it well
- Plain, human language. Short sentences. Benefits and problems in the buyer's words.
- No buzzwords ("decision-maker persona", "synergy", "leverage", "pain points galore", "seamless").
- Specific beats general: "has to pass a new EU label rule by June" beats "needs compliance".
- Never invent customers, numbers, quotes or survey results. Unproven items are marked as hypotheses to validate.

## 8. Visualize
Build one board. If a Design or Dashboard artifact type is listed, use it; otherwise one HTML artifact. Brand colours of the client if known, otherwise Byzantine purple (#702963), near-black (#1F1A1E), light tint (#F6F1F5) and Montserrat. Flat, minimal, lots of white space; no stock photos, avatars, emoji or gradients. Read the dataviz skill before drawing charts.

Sections, top to bottom:
1. **ICP card**: one line on who the ideal customer is, then the firmographics scorecard, signals, disqualifiers and the fit score.
2. **Buying committee map**: a power × interest grid (or an org-style map). Each role is a labelled dot, shaped or labelled by its role in the deal; the champion and decision maker in purple, others in grey; external influencers outside the company boundary, joined to the roles they influence.
3. **Buying journey**: a horizontal timeline of the stages. Under each stage: active roles (small labels), what they do, the key question, the content that helps, and where they look. The stall point is marked.
4. **Persona cards**: one card per key role, side by side, with goals, pains, triggers, objections with replies, criteria, where to find them and the opening line.
5. **Where to find them**: a channel table, channels × personas, marking which channel reaches which role, ranked by priority.
6. **Sources and hypotheses**, collapsed at the bottom.

Add a "Copy" button on each persona card and on the ICP card so text can be pasted into CRM, decks and briefs.

## 9. Check before showing
- [ ] The ICP is specific enough that sales could build a list of 50 companies from it
- [ ] Every pain, trigger and objection has a source or is marked as a hypothesis
- [ ] The buying committee includes finance or procurement, the end user and at least one external influencer
- [ ] Each journey stage names the roles involved and the content that helps
- [ ] "Where to find them" names real channels, events and publications
- [ ] No invented names, photos, quotes or numbers
- [ ] Persona swap test: if two persona cards could swap titles without anyone noticing, sharpen them

## 10. Handoff
In the reply: the ICP in one line, the champion and the decision maker, the stall point in the journey, the top 3 channels, and the hypotheses to validate (suggest 5 customer interviews). Offer one next step: a messaging house for the lead persona, a sales battlecard, or a target account list of 25 companies that fit the ICP.
