# Sales battlecards

Goal: cards a seller can use live, in the 30 seconds before or during a call, to win against a named competitor or against "do nothing". One card per competitor, plus one for the status quo. Every claim is true, sourced, dated and ready to say out loud.

Built on current practice:
- **Know, Say, Show** (Klue): what the seller must know, the words to say, the proof to show. Most cards fail on the last two; audits found few cards include talk tracks or customer-facing proof.
- **Fact, Impact, Act**: every point states a fact, why it matters to this buyer, and what the seller does with it.
- **Quick dismiss and landmines**: a short, fair line that knocks a competitor off the shortlist early, and questions planted later that expose a real gap.
- **Why we win, why we lose**: honest, from win/loss evidence, not wishful thinking.
- **Status quo is a competitor**: many qualified deals end in "no decision", not a loss to a rival.
- **Usable, fresh, measured**: one strong card per competitor beats many weak ones; stale cards are worse than none; success is competitive win rate, not page views.

## 1. Brief (ask only what's missing, max 4 questions, in one message)
Inputs:
- **Our company and product** (website, deck, or the positioning and messaging house if they exist)
- **Competitors** to cover (if none named, find the 3–5 the buyer most often compares with, and add "do nothing / build it in-house")
- **Industry and segment** (optional). If the person names an industry, apply the industry lens in step 3. If not, stay industry-neutral and say so.
- **Internal intel** if available: win/loss notes, CRM loss reasons, call recordings or notes, sales Slack threads, RFP answers. Internal intel is usually the best intel, so always ask for it once.

If nobody is there to answer, make reasonable assumptions, list them at the top, and continue.

## 2. Research each competitor
Search each competitor separately. Open the page before using it; never summarise from a snippet.
- **Their story**: home page, product pages, "vs" and comparison pages they publish about us, ads, recent launches, release notes, pricing page, packaging
- **Proof they use**: case studies, logos, awards, certifications
- **What buyers say**: review sites (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Trustpilot, Google, app stores, or the industry's own review venues), forums, community threads, social posts. Look for repeated praise and repeated complaints.
- **Company signals**: funding, financial results, layoffs, leadership changes, hiring (job posts reveal strategy and gaps), partnerships, acquisitions, legal or regulatory issues
- **Their pitch against us**: what their sellers likely say about us (from their comparison pages, analyst notes, our lost-deal notes)
- **Win/loss evidence**: why we won and lost against them, in the buyer's words

Keep a source and a date next to every fact. Rate each fact's confidence: **Verified** (primary source or several sources), **Reported** (one credible source), **Field intel** (our sellers or partners, unverified).

## 3. Industry lens (only when an industry is named)
Adapt the cards to how that industry buys. Examples:
- **Regulated (pharma, medical devices, food supplements, finance, insurance)**: claims must be approved by the company's own review process; comparisons stick to documented specs, approvals and certifications; no off-label or unapproved health or performance claims.
- **Manufacturing, chemicals, ingredients, industrial equipment**: specifications, total cost of ownership, quality and audit history, supply security, lead times, technical service, local stock.
- **Software and SaaS**: integrations, security and compliance certificates, implementation time, admin effort, pricing model, roadmap credibility.
- **Professional services and agencies**: team and expertise, method, references, commercial model, speed to value.
- **Public sector and tenders**: award criteria and weighting, mandatory requirements, references, framework agreements; battlecards map to the scoring grid.
- **Consumer and retail brands selling to trade**: sell-through data, margin, promotional support, logistics, category story.

State the lens at the top of each card: "Industry lens: [industry]" or "Industry-neutral".

## 4. Build the card (one per competitor)
**Front: the 30-second view (fits one screen or one A4 side)**
1. **Header**: competitor name, one-line description, "Last verified: [date]", owner.
2. **In one line**: how to beat them, e.g. "Win on [our difference] for [buyer type]; avoid head-to-head on [their strength]."
3. **When you meet them**: segments, deal sizes, regions, triggers where they show up most.
4. **Their pitch** (in their words, 1–2 lines) and **what they say about us**, with our reply.
5. **Why we win** (top 3): each as Fact → Impact → Act, with one proof point.
6. **Why we lose** (top 2, honest) and how to avoid each.
7. **Quick dismiss**: one or two sentences: acknowledge a real strength, name the gap, contrast with our value. Fair and true.
8. **Landmines** (2–3): discovery questions that surface a need they cannot meet well. Phrase them as genuine questions about the buyer's needs, never as attacks.

**Back: depth for preparation**
9. **Objections they trigger**: the 3–5 objections buyers raise when this competitor is in the deal, each with a short response (use the objection-handling method: acknowledge, explore, respond, confirm).
10. **Landmine defense**: the traps they set for us, and how to defuse each.
11. **Strengths and weaknesses**: honest, short, sourced. Include where they are genuinely better.
12. **Pricing and packaging**: their model, list prices if public, typical discounts if known (marked Field intel), and how to frame our price against theirs (value and total cost, not just sticker price).
13. **Proof to show**: customer stories, switching stories, third-party reviews, certifications, demo moments. Mark each **Customer-safe** or **Internal only**.
14. **Talk tracks**: 3–5 lines a seller can say word for word, each 2 sentences or fewer.
15. **Don'ts**: what never to say (unverifiable claims, disparaging remarks, confidential information).
16. **Sources**: every fact with source, date and confidence.

**Always add the "Do nothing" card**: why buyers stay put, the cost of inaction in the buyer's terms, the fear of making the wrong choice, and how to make the decision feel safe (pilots, phased rollouts, guarantees, references from similar buyers).

## 5. Write it well
- Plain words a seller can say on a call. Short sentences. No jargon the buyer wouldn't use.
- Lead with buyer outcomes, not feature checklists. A feature only matters if it changes a result the buyer cares about.
- Fair and factual about competitors. Acknowledge their real strengths; it builds trust and makes the contrast believable. Comparative claims must be true, verifiable and not misleading; customer-facing comparisons may need legal or regulatory review.
- No buzzwords ("best-in-class", "leverage", "seamless", "game-changing").
- Never invent facts, prices, customers, quotes or win rates. If unknown, leave a visible gap like [Their current list price] and say where to find it.

## 6. Visualize
Use a Design or Dashboard artifact type if listed and fitting; otherwise one HTML artifact. Brand colours of the client if known, otherwise Byzantine purple (#702963), near-black (#1F1A1E), light tint (#F6F1F5) and Montserrat. Flat and minimal, no stock images or emoji.
- One tab or section per competitor plus "Do nothing", with an overview strip on top: each competitor's one-line "how to beat them" and threat level.
- Each card shows the front first (one screen), the back below or behind a toggle.
- Visual cues: Why we win and Why we lose side by side; quick dismiss and landmines in highlighted boxes; confidence tags (Verified, Reported, Field intel) and Customer-safe / Internal-only tags on every item; "Last verified" date on every card.
- A "Copy" button on each talk track, quick dismiss and landmine.
- Print-friendly: each card front fits one A4 or Letter page.

## 7. Check before showing
- [ ] Each card answers "how do I beat them" in one line at the top
- [ ] Every claim has a source, date and confidence tag; nothing invented
- [ ] Why we lose is filled honestly, not left out
- [ ] Each "why we win" has a proof point a seller can show
- [ ] Quick dismisses and landmines are fair, true and phrased around the buyer's needs
- [ ] Talk tracks are short enough to say out loud
- [ ] A "Do nothing" card exists
- [ ] Industry lens applied if an industry was named; otherwise marked industry-neutral
- [ ] Customer-facing items are marked and contain no confidential information

## 8. Handoff
In the reply: the one-line "how to beat them" for each competitor, the biggest risk (where we lose most), the gaps to fill (pricing, win/loss evidence), and a maintenance plan:
- **Owner** and **review cadence** (monthly for top competitors, quarterly for others), plus triggers for an immediate update (their launch, price change, funding, a big loss).
- **Measure**: competitive win rate per competitor before and after, card use in deals, seller feedback.
- **Feedback loop**: after each competitive deal, the seller logs what worked and what was outdated.

Offer one next step: the objection-handling playbook, a win/loss interview guide, or a customer-facing comparison page.
