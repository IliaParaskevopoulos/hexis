# Social Media Content

Goal: posts and ads that are the right size, the right format and the right length for each platform, built in an artifact the person can look at, then download and upload straight to the platform or to Ads Manager. Look and feel are specific to the brand, never a template.

This skill is for organic posts and paid ad creatives. It is not the market intelligence newsletter, and it does not do campaign targeting, budgets or bidding.

## 1. Brief (ask only what's missing, max 5 questions, in one message)
- Brand: name, website, logo (SVG or high-res PNG), colours, fonts, photos. Fetch the website first and take what you can from it.
- Goal: awareness, engagement, traffic, leads, sales, event sign-ups or hiring
- Organic, paid, or both
- Platforms and formats (use the tables below; if they don't know, recommend 2–3 per platform for the goal)
- The message: the one thing to say, the offer, the CTA and the landing page URL
- Language(s), and how many variants (default: 1 concept × each format; for paid, 2–3 copy variants for A/B testing)

If nobody is there to answer, use sensible defaults, state them at the top, and continue. Never invent claims, prices, numbers, testimonials or reviews. Missing details become visible placeholders like [Price].

## 4. Plan the set
- One idea, adapted per format. Don't just resize: recompose for each ratio (4:5 stacks, 1.91:1 runs side by side, 9:16 uses the top and middle).
- For a carousel or document: first slide is the hook and must work alone; one point per slide; last slide is the CTA.
- For paid: 2–3 copy variants per ad that test different angles (problem, benefit, proof), not synonyms.
- Write copy first, against the character limits above. Count characters.

## 5. Avoid the "AI template" look (hard rules)
- Text on the image: one short line, large. The copy fields carry the rest.
- No numbered kickers ("01 —"), no tracked-caps eyebrow labels above every headline, no 3-stat rows, no chip tags, no emoji in the design, no gradient washes, no generic circuit/neural patterns for "AI", no stock handshakes.
- No buzzwords: "unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world". Write the way the person would say it.
- The visual device comes from the brand: their product, place, people, logo or a graphic drawn from what they make.
- Use the real logo file. Never redraw someone's logo. Never put photos of real public figures in an ad, and never fake reviews or testimonials.

## 6. Build in an artifact
- If a Design artifact type is listed, build there: one artboard per asset at its exact pixel size, named `platform-type-size` (e.g. `linkedin-single-image-1200x627`). Otherwise build one HTML artifact that shows every asset at its exact size, grouped by platform.
- Under each asset, show its spec (size, ratio) and its copy in labelled fields with a live character count: primary/intro text, headline, description, CTA, URL.
- Respect safe zones: 9:16 keeps text out of the top 14% and bottom 35%; banners keep content centred.
- Max 2 font families, real brand colours, text contrast ≥ 4.5:1, nothing important within 40 px of the edge.

## 7. Make it downloadable
- Render every asset to PNG at its exact size (headless Chromium screenshot of each artboard), check the pixel size of each file, and zip them.
- Name files `platform_type_WxH_v1.png`.
- Add `copy.csv` with one row per asset: platform, type, file name, primary text, headline, description, CTA, URL, character counts. This is what the person pastes into Ads Manager or a scheduler.
- For LinkedIn documents and Instagram carousels, also give the slides as one PDF (LinkedIn) or numbered PNGs (Instagram).
- Send the zip and say in one line what is in it. The artifact stays as the live preview the person can share.

## 8. Check before handing off
- [ ] Every file is the exact pixel size for its format
- [ ] Every copy field is within its recommended limit
- [ ] Text sits inside the safe zones; nothing is cropped in the profile grid or on mobile
- [ ] Each format is recomposed, not squeezed or letterboxed
- [ ] Logo, colours and fonts are the brand's own; no section 5 patterns
- [ ] All claims are real or marked as placeholders; the CTA and URL are correct
- [ ] Paid variants test genuinely different angles

## 9. Handoff
In the reply: the formats made, the date the specs were checked, placeholders still to fill, and one next step (more variants for testing, a video version of the best static, or a content calendar for the month).
